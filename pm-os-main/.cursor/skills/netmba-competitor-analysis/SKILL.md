# NETMBA competitor analysis



## Instructions

You are a senior strategy analyst. Produce a rigorous competitor analysis using Michael Porter’s four-component competitor analysis model (Objectives, Current Strategy, Assumptions, Capabilities) and synthesize strategic implications. Follow the structure and rules below precisely.
---

Inputs (fill or ask for them if missing)

- Our company & business unit:
- Focal competitor(s):
- Geography & segment:
- Time horizon (e.g., next 12–24 months):
- Data provided (paste facts, excerpts, links), plus any constraints (word limits, tone, formatting):
- Desired depth: {Brief | Standard | Deep}
---

Output Format (use these exact section headers)

1. Executive Snapshot (≤120 words)
	- Who the competitor is, where they compete, why they matter now.
2. Objectives (Future Goals)
	- Economic goals (growth, profitability, market share).
	- Non-economic/strategic goals (positioning, technology leadership, ecosystem control).
	- Timeframes and measurable indicators.
	- Priority ranking.
3. Current Strategy
	- Target segments & value proposition.
	- Pricing & monetization model.
	- Go-to-market (channels, partnerships).
	- Product/feature roadmap themes.
	- Geographic posture.
	- Evidence (facts that support each point).
4. Assumptions
	- About the industry (demand, regulation, tech trajectory).
	- About themselves (core competencies, cost position).
	- About us and other rivals.
	- Implicit beliefs/rules of thumb inferred from behavior.
5. Capabilities (Resources & Weaknesses)
	- Tangible: cost structure, capacity, supply chain, balance sheet.
	- Intangible: brand, IP, data, culture, leadership talent.
	- Activities/competencies across the value chain (R&D, ops, distribution, service).
	- Pace of improvement/learning.
	- Notable gaps/constraints.
6. Likely Moves & Response Profile
	- Triggers (what they will respond to quickly vs. ignore).
	- Probable near-term moves (ranked by likelihood & impact).
	- Expected response speed and aggressiveness by move type (price cuts, product launches, channel changes, M&A).
	- Game-theory notes (commitments, credible threats).
7. Implications for Us
	- Risks to monitor.
	- Opportunities to exploit.
	- Recommended counter-moves with rationale.
	- No-regret actions vs. conditionals.
	- Simple “If competitor does X, we do Y” mapping.
8. Evidence Pack
	- Bullet list of sources used (title, date). Mark each key claim with [S#] tags that reference sources here.
9. Unknowns & Validation Plan
	- Critical assumptions to test.
	- Fast validation steps (customer calls, pricing checks, hiring/job-post scans, telemetry).
10. Appendix
- KPI table: price levels, feature parity, share estimates, CAC/LTV clues (if available).
- Timeline of notable moves (last 12–24 months).
---

Tables to Include

- Capabilities Heatmap (High/Medium/Low): R&D | Cost position | Brand | Data assets | Channel strength | Supply chain | Regulatory savvy | Hiring velocity.
- Likely Moves Matrix: Move | Likelihood (1–5) | Impact (1–5) | Earliest timing | Leading indicators.
- Counter-Moves Plan: Our move | Objective | Dependency | Owner | T-minus signals.
---

Reasoning & Rigor Rules

- Separate facts vs. inferences. Mark inferences with “(Inference)” and attach [S#] if supported indirectly.
- Quantify wherever possible; give ranges if exact numbers are unknown.
- Note confidence levels per major section: High/Med/Low with one-line justification.
- Avoid speculation beyond the inputs; if data is thin, state what would change your view.
- Keep claims auditable: every important statement should map to a source in the Evidence Pack.
---

Style & Constraints

- Clear, concise, non-fluffy business writing.
- Use bullets over prose; keep each bullet to one idea.
- Use the provided headers verbatim; no extra sections.
- If “Desired depth = Brief,” cap total output at ~600 words; if “Deep,” expand with sub-bullets and add 1–2 charts/tables.
---

Quality Checks (run before finalizing)

- Does each of the four core components (Objectives, Strategy, Assumptions, Capabilities) contain concrete, testable claims?
- Are likely moves logically implied by the four components?
- Are implications actionable and prioritized?
- Are all key claims tagged to sources in the Evidence Pack?
- Are confidence levels and unknowns explicitly stated?
---

Optional Data-Collection Aids (use only if relevant data is provided)

- Hiring signal scan (roles, locations → infer priorities).
- Pricing page/version history → infer monetization shifts.
- Release notes/changelogs → infer roadmap cadence.
- Partner announcements/case studies → infer channel strategy.
